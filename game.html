Dungeon Crawler Caravan

This is the other thing I do when I'm not driving or listening to something. I'm making a game. It runs, it's playable, and it's the first thing I've built that I'd actually let somebody else sit down in front of.

Short version: it's a Vampire Survivors-style crawl with a Dungeon Crawler Carl coat of paint. You move, your weapons fire themselves, the screen fills up with things trying to kill you, and every level-up hands you three cards and makes you pick one. Fifteen minutes to the win — and then it doesn't stop.

Why this, of all things

Dungeon Crawler Carl is my favorite series on this whole site, and the thing that stuck with me hardest isn't the loot or the levels. It's the System — the way it keeps tightening the screws while narrating your beating back to you like it's entertainment. That's a feeling, and it turns out a Vampire Survivors run is shaped almost exactly right for it. One guy, a horde that never stops arriving, and something out there that finds the whole thing very funny.

So the run doesn't end when you win. Surviving the full fifteen minutes banks the victory and drops you into overtime, which doesn't end until you die. Stopping a run at the exact moment your build finally comes online is the one thing that wastes the build — so instead the clock goes amber and the brakes come off.

[The joke escalates with the threat] After the two scheduled bosses, they just keep coming, and they get named instead of numbered. First "TWO WATCHERS". Then "A CONCERNING NUMBER OF WATCHERS". Then "MANAGEMENT".

What's actually in it

7characters, one innate ability each
9auto-firing weapons, six equip slots
9evolutions, each behind its own passive
3mythics, offered rather than given
5regions on an endless map
15minutes to the win, then forever

The stuff I'm happiest with, in no real order:

  • Nine weapons against six slots. You never get to carry the whole set, so what you leave behind is a decision instead of a shopping list.
  • Every passive gates exactly one evolution. That was the fix for a real problem — two of the passives used to gate nothing, which made them the only cards on a level-up screen that couldn't be part of a plan.
  • The mythics come with a Take It / Leave It prompt, because they're decisions, not pickups. One of them is x100 damage dealt, and you take double. It either wins the run or ends it inside ten seconds.
  • Two enemies exist purely to break the pattern. The Brute winds up, flashes white, and commits to a dash it can't re-aim — so you leave the lane instead of out-damaging it. The Sapper is fragile, fast, and detonates on death across faction lines, so one killed in the middle of the horde does your work for you and one killed at your feet does not.
  • The floor remembers. Every death leaves a stain in that creature's own blood colour and every bomb leaves a scorch. At overtime crowd sizes, clean ground is the loudest possible signal that nothing you're doing has any weight.
  • Ruined villages that stay put. Roughly one chunk in seven is a village, hashed off its own coordinates — walk away, come back, the same houses are standing in the same places.
  • Birds that leave when you walk at them. They can't be hurt and they do nothing. That's the point: a bird standing still is set dressing, a bird that takes off because of you is the world reacting, which is most of what "alive" means in a game that otherwise only reacts by attacking.

And then there's Protente

Six of the seven characters are about what you'd expect out of a roster like this — a soldier, a ghost, a guy with a big shield. And then there's my sticker guy.

Protente has been kicking around my stuff for years. A screaming goat in armor holding a soda, and the other one that just says there will be no more warm hugs from me. Once I had a character select screen, putting him on it wasn't really a decision.

So he's a black satyr, and his ability is Scream — every enemy in earshot gets stunned for two seconds. Obviously it's the scream. He's also the most fragile character in the game and the physically largest, 21x30 against everyone else's 17x22, so he's a bigger target carrying less HP. That's deliberate: he's the only crowd control on the roster, and that ought to cost something.

Level him up far enough and the scream runs longer, then covers most of the screen, then starts doing damage on its own. No more warm hugs, exactly as advertised.

Where it's actually at

Honest answer: the whole thing runs end to end, and almost none of it has been properly played.

Balance is untested. I've done a tuning pass, but it came off the numbers — nobody has sat down and played the characters against each other for real. The escalation curve past the win is completely unplayed. The crowd ceiling is 900 enemies, and that's a guess at what the machine can push, not anything I measured.

There's a debug overlay on F1 with the frame rate on it, and that's the first number I'll be watching in a late overtime crowd. If it can't hold, the ceiling comes down.

[Known rough edge] You can't currently stop at the win. Surviving fifteen minutes banks it, but the only way out is dying or quitting from the pause menu. It needs a "claim victory" option instead of falling straight through into overtime.

Under the hood, briefly

GameMaker, pure GML, no shaders and no engine plugins. Native resolution is 640x360, upscaled to whatever whole multiple fits your display, because pixel art at a fractional scale looks like garbage.

The lighting is a light map built on a surface each frame and multiplied over the finished picture — the torch you carry, plus fire, explosions, lightning, orbitals, chests and bosses all casting their own colour. Everything that could be data is data: characters, abilities, weapons and upgrades all live in tables, so adding a new one touches no object code.
