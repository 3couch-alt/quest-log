12 Miles Below: Cool World, Middling Payoff

Mark Arrows' 12 Miles Below drops you on a future Earth where the surface has frozen into a deadly ice waste, and humanity's split between fragile surface clans and whatever's survived in the depths below. It follows Keith Winterscar and his father after an expedition goes wrong — and the setup alone is one of the more striking premises I've come across in the genre.

The best part is the world

The Depths aren't just tunnels — it's a massive, shifting subterranean maze that behaves more like a living machine than solid rock, packed with scavenged tech, rune-like systems, and things that are less "monster" and more "demigod." Treating high-tech sci-fi elements like a magic system is a neat trick, and the surface-versus-depths contrast gives the whole series a distinct identity.

[Where it lost me a bit] The concept and setting carry a lot of weight here — the weak-to-strong growth and survival stakes are all present and correct, but it didn't grab me on a character level the way my favorites do. Good, not great.

Worth a listen for the world-building alone if frozen-apocalypse settings and scavenged-tech progression sound like your thing. Just didn't climb higher than a 6/10 for me overall.
