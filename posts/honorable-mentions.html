Honorable Mentions: Five Series Outside My Usual Lane

A few series that hit me hard even though they're nowhere near my usual LitRPG lane. Figured they earned a mention.

The Dark Tower — Stephen King (8/10)

This series is an absolute beast — dark fantasy, western, and horror all mashed together, which isn't my usual territory, but damn if it didn't stick with me anyway. Roland the gunslinger is on a quest that feels bigger than life itself. The atmosphere is haunting as hell and the scope is massive. This is the kind of series that gets under your skin and just stays there.

Super Powereds — Drew Hayes (7.5/10)

Another curveball — a superhero series set at a college with a Hero Certification Program. The twist is it's about a group of "Powereds," people with superpowers they can't control, basically the underdogs compared to the "Supers" who've got their shit figured out. What makes it work is the found-family vibe and the college dynamic. The Powereds get a secret shot at fixing their control so they can pass as Supers, and hiding that from everyone is just genuinely fun to watch.

Scythe — Neal Shusterman (7/10)

A YA dystopian pick, and a genuinely fucked-up premise: humanity beat death, so "scythes" are the only ones left with the job of permanently killing people to keep the population in check. Following two teen apprentices getting forced into learning that trade is a hell of a hook, and the world Shusterman builds around it — perfect on the surface, ice-cold underneath — sticks with you. Not as immersive as a good LitRPG binge, but a solid listen for a change of pace.

The Name of the Wind — Patrick Rothfuss (9/10)

Kvothe telling his own damn legend to a chronicler in a nothing little inn shouldn't work as well as it does, but Rothfuss makes you believe every fucking word of it. The prose is stupid good — dense enough that it could've been a slog, except it never is. Music, sympathy and naming and the whole University system, a kid who's a legend and a fuck-up at the same time — it's a slow burn, but it earns every hour it asks for.

[Narration note] Nick Podehl carries this one completely. His read is a huge part of why Kvothe's voice lands as hard as it does — the man's basically synonymous with this series at this point.

Ready Player One — Ernest Cline (8.5/10)

A dying billionaire hides his entire fortune inside a global VR system and turns the whole damn planet into 80s-trivia-obsessed egg hunters — Wade Watts chasing that prize through old games and dead pop culture is just a fun-as-hell premise, full stop. Nostalgia-bait in the best way, and the stakes never feel fake even when the setup gets a little silly.

[Narration note] Wil Wheaton narrates this one, not Podehl, and it's a perfect match — dude practically lived through the same 80s shit Cline's constantly name-dropping, so it never sounds like he's reading off a script he doesn't get.
[Why mention these here] All five are way outside my usual LitRPG zone, but they pull off the same trick the genre I actually cover does — they get under your skin and don't let go. Worth checking out even if LitRPG isn't your thing.
